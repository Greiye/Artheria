**The Cycle of Balance**  
In this system, the universe is in a constant state of equilibrium. Entities are finite, and when one is destroyed, its essence is transmuted into another form elsewhere in the world. This keeps the total number of entities at a constant level.

### **Magic’s Law of Conservation**

Magic in this world behaves similarly to the law of energy conservation. It cannot be destroyed, only transformed. The key difference is that magic can be converted over vast distances, even across the globe. This principle forms the backbone of the world’s magical ecosystem.

### **Entities**

There are 100 limited entities in this world. When an entity is destroyed, it reappears in another form. Entities are ranked by points, which represent their significance and influence.

### **Portal (5 Points)**

Portals allow access to other planes of existence. Each portal is worth 5 points and closes after these 5 points are used. Resources or items can emerge from the portal, but once the points are exhausted, the portal shuts.

### **Resources**

All magic stems from resources. These resources are the core of the world’s economy, magic system, and conflicts.

**Magic Types:**

- **Gods and Artifacts**
    
    - Have nested properties.
        
    - Can join or separate entities.
        
    - Act as remainders and are able to perform the following:
        
        - A: Destroy portals.
            
        - B: Destroy creatures or groups.
            
        - C: Destroy cities.
            
        - D: Destroy powerful magic items.
            
        - E: Rare items that can shift the course of battles.
            

### **Hero Tiers**

Heroes are classified by their level and corresponding class:

- Level 3: Class E
    
- Level 5: Class D
    
- Level 10: Class C
    
- Level 15: Class B
    
- Level 20: Class A

### **Story Combat: The Magic Law of Conservation**

When co-DMs collaborate, disagreements may arise. Perhaps one DM kills your favorite character or weakens a beloved race. In response, you can engage in **Story Combat**, where lore and creativity serve as weapons.

In this world, magic abides by the **Law of Conservation**, which means no new entity can be introduced without destroying an existing one. Every entity has a set number of points. The sum total of these points is always 100.

If you destroy an entity worth 5 points, the world’s point balance shifts from 100 to 95. You can now introduce a new entity worth 5 points, keeping the world in balance. This mechanism ensures that creativity and destruction are always in tension, maintaining a dynamic but finite ecosystem.

---

### **Example of Story Combat: Dragons vs. Tieflings**

Let’s say Dragons (worth 2 points) and Tieflings (worth 3 points) coexist in the world. As a DM, you want to eradicate dragons to empower your tiefling race. Through story, you seek to eliminate the 2-point dragons, leaving you with 3 tieflings and 0 dragons. The 3(Tieflings) – 2(Dragons) = 1 point left, meaning the death of the dragons also costs the tieflings their lives, with only 1 remaining survivor to allow repopulation.

Your co-DM, however, wants to preserve the dragon race. They can strengthen their dragons by aligning them with other entities or using magical artifacts. In this way, **Story Combat** unfolds, where each DM fights for the survival of their lore through strategic use of points and entities.

---

### **Fair Reintroduction**

If your lore is successfully wiped out by another DM’s actions, you can reintroduce your lore by using the available points. For example, after dragons are wiped out, they can be reintroduced through a magic artifact such as dragon eggs. This keeps the balance fair and provides ongoing narrative challenges.

By maintaining the integrity of the **Law of Magic Conservation**, every DM can create, destroy, and reshape the world while respecting the contributions of others. This method encourages collaboration and conflict resolution through storytelling and strategic lore-building.
