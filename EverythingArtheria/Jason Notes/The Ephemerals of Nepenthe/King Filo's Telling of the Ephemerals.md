_A tale spoken in the glow of torchlight to a circle of velvet-robed women, wine swirling, the wind outside howling from the peak of the volcano._

> “Ah, my darlings... lean close. I will tell you a story few dare to whisper on these cliffs, for fear the wind might carry their words to a god who listens from beneath the stone.
> 
> The story of **Anarch the Ephemeral** — the first king, the founder of this very peak, and the one who may yet burn it all to ash.”

He paces slowly, rapier at his hip, voice warm and haunting.+

> “Before me, before Castle Blackthorn, before dragons called this mountain home... there was **Anarch**. A child of rebellion. A friend once, to kindness and reason. But he did not stay so.
> 
> You see, Anarch drank from the **Seed of Tithonus** — a tree born from a man cursed to live forever without youth. But Anarch... he was clever. He claimed both life and youth in that sap, and from it, he became a god.”

A pause. He raises a glass.

> “And gods... grow lonely.”

> “With time, Anarch tamed dragons, built empires, and crushed kingdoms beneath his immortal hand. He became the **Deity of Lachesism** — chaos incarnate. But in the silence of eternity, he learned a bitter truth:
> 
> **Power means nothing, when no one can match you.**
> 
> So he vanished, my darlings. He walked into myth. But he left behind a prophecy. A whisper.”

He gestures grandly — the room chills.

> “**The Grande Sonnerie** — The Great Striking. The final toll of the god-king’s will. It is said that one day, Anarch will return. Not to rule. Not to speak. But to destroy all of creation in a single blow.
> 
> And that, my loves, will be the last note in his symphony of chaos.”

He leans in with a roguish grin, eyes gleaming.

> “Unless, of course, someone stops him.”

A playful wink. But the firelight flickers darker now, as if the mountain itself had heard the name and stirred beneath them.
