
_A myth of youth, rebellion, and the eternal price of immortality._

---

### **Chapter 1: The Island of Nepenthe**

There was once an island cloaked in wonder, a hidden haven called **Nepenthe**. It thrived in isolation — a place where life sparkled with vitality. Its people lived long, joyful lives, nourished by lush gardens and radiant fruit, seemingly untouched by the ravages of time. That was, until the **[[Arrestors]]** arrived.

Driven by ambition, the Arrestors were conquistadors, merciless and hungry for power. They invaded the island, enslaving its native people in pursuit of an ancient legend: the **Seed of Tithonus** — a mythical relic believed to grant eternal youth.

---

### **Chapter 2: The Curse of Tithonus**

Long ago, a mortal man named **[[Thithonus]]** was granted eternal life, but not eternal youth. Time ravaged his body, trapping his mind in a decaying shell. In a desperate effort to undo his curse, he created a seed rumored to reverse aging — a seed said to be hidden on Nepenthe.

The Arrestors believed that with the seed, they could achieve immortality and build an empire without end. And so, they tore across the island in search of it, leaving ruin in their wake.

---

### **Chapter 3: The Young Aasimar Trio**

Amidst the chaos, three young Aasimars rose from among the enslaved:

- **[[Anarch]]**, defiant and impulsive, who rejected the life forced upon him.
    
- **[[Vellichor]]**, his quiet, logical companion and voice of reason.
    
- And **[[Petrichor]]**, Anarch’s sister, a radiant spirit full of compassion and wonder.
    

They refused to accept a future written by the Arrestors. Inspired by the legend of the seed and a dream Petrichor held dear — of a glowing, immortal tree — the trio set out to find it, not for power, but for freedom.

---

### **Chapter 4: The Evergreen Sapling**

Their search led them through every corner of Nepenthe until they found it: a colossal tree with glowing leaves and a towering trunk — just as Petrichor had described. Though ancient in form, it was still just a sapling. Vellichor, ever the pragmatist, believed it was their only chance of escape.

Unbeknownst to them, an Arrestor had tracked their every move.

---

### **Chapter 5: Leap of Faith**

Anarch sensed they were being followed. Upon confirming it, he acted swiftly. With courage and desperation, he stabbed the tree, coating his blade with its glowing sap. Without hesitation, he leapt off the cliffside and into the sea below.

Vellichor and Petrichor, urged by Anarch, collected as much sap as they could. Just as the Arrestor revealed himself, Vellichor handed Petrichor a vial. Together, they drank — and jumped.

---

### **Chapter 6: The Birth of the Ephemerals**

The Arrestor reached the edge, expecting to see bodies dashed against the rocks or floating lifeless in the ocean. But there was nothing. No movement. No sign of survival.

He returned to his leader and reported their fate. The trio, he claimed, had vanished — nothing more than ephemeral sparks extinguished by the wind. And thus, the name **"The Ephemerals"** was born, whispered with derision.

But deep below the surface, bubbles broke through the waves. Three figures swam to shore — alive, glowing faintly, transformed. In escaping death, they had found true life.

That day marked the birth of legend: **The Ephemerals of Nepenthe** — living proof that courage, hope, and sacrifice could defy even time itself.

### **Chapter 7: The Ephemeral King**

The trio emerged from the depths changed — glowing, eternal, and free. While Petrichor and Vellichor remained driven by compassion and reason, something inside Anarch began to shift. His rebellion, once forged in survival, curdled into ambition.

Legends say it was Anarch who led the others to a distant land — a place that would become known as **Amethyst Peak**. There, he declared himself ruler: **King Anarch**, the first to wield the gifts of Nepenthe beyond its shores.

He ruled with unmatched might and unshakable resolve. Time could not touch him, and armies bowed beneath his will. But power isolated him. The boy who had once leapt to protect his friends became **“The King Who Cared for No One.”**

In time, myths called him **The Ephemeral** — not for his short-lived nature, but for the fleeting humanity he once held.

Worse still, his legacy bore a curse:  
the **Sky Dweller's Curse** — a divine affliction passed down through his bloodline and those who reached too far for power.  
A curse of distance. Detachment. A god above the clouds, watching but never weeping.

And so, Anarch’s story became a tragedy sung in whispers:  
A leap for freedom...  
A crown of thorns...  
A god who forgot he was once just a boy with a blade and a dream.

### **Epilogue: The Grande Sonnerie**

Long after the Ephemerals vanished into legend, and the age of mortals gave way to kingdoms and crowns, one name echoed louder than the rest: **Anarch** — the God of Lachesism, the Deity of Power and Chaos, the First King of Amethyst Peak.

His ascent was marked by triumph:  
He founded a kingdom in the skies, forged **Castle Blackthorn** from stone and starlight, and commanded the tides of war like a symphony.

But immortality weighs heavy on the soul.

Though granted the eternal youth and life promised by the **Seed of Tithonus**, Anarch came to see the gift for what it truly was:  
a **curse**.

What is power, if none can challenge you?  
What is existence, if no one dares defy you?  
What is eternity, if it drowns out meaning?

And so, from within the highest spire of his creation, Anarch came to a final conclusion — whispered in madness and resolve:

> “If I cannot be moved, then all must be silenced.”

This thought birthed the prophecy of [[The Grande Sonnerie]]  — the Great Striking, the final toll that would signal the end of all things. It is said Anarch vanished soon after, leaving no body, no blade, no word.

Only silence.

But silence is not the same as absence.

Across time, a belief has grown in secret circles and fearful prayers: that one day, Anarch will return — not as king, nor god, but as the end itself. A living paradox. A being for whom absolute power became an absolute void.

> **“Absolute power corrupts absolutely.”**  
> And when the bell tolls, not even the divine will be spared.
