He didn’t browse. Just tapped the menu with a single finger.

“Espresso. No milk.”

His voice was low, even-toned—efficient. He didn’t look rushed, only certain. The kind of certainty that came from routine, not impulse. His eyes lingered on the menu carved into driftwood above the bar—blotched with pressed flower ink and salt-patina—before returning to the notebook in his lap. Numbers lined the page in neat columns. He didn’t fidget. He didn’t scroll. He just _was_.

The barista—bright-eyed, ink-smudged, and barefoot on the worn floorboards—tilted her head, arms resting on the counter. “You know,” she offered, “you might like the Bloomleaf.”

He looked up, eyebrows raised slightly. “Is that a pastry?”

She laughed. “No, it’s a drink. Like tea, but… creamier. Sweeter. It's made to be sipped.”

He studied her for a moment—not judgmental, just… still. Observing.

“I prefer drinks with a function,” he said. “I’m trying to wake up, not fall in love.”

She grinned. “Why not both?”

The café was nestled into a quiet alcove of Aralon’s hillside—a space made of sun-warped wood and fluttering paper lanterns. It smelled of sea salt and matcha steam, and the rain pattering on the ceiling tiles made everything feel like it was happening inside a dream.

He sat near the window, where light caught on the rim of his cup and cast little gold circles onto the table. His espresso arrived: small, dark, no-nonsense.

She came back too.

He noticed, but didn’t say anything. Not until she sat across from him, cradling a bowl of something pale green and velvety, like whipped grass.

“That’s Bloomleaf?” he asked.

She nodded. “Sweetgrass, steamed oatmilk, hint of blueflower salt. Locals say it calms the nerves.”

He gave a small, half-smile—the first real one she saw. “That’s why I avoid it.”

She raised an eyebrow. “You have something against calm?”

“No,” he said. “I just don’t like being tricked by pretty things.”

Her grin widened. “So I should assume you find me _very_ suspicious.”

His smile deepened—just slightly, the corner of his mouth tipping. “Extremely.”

They talked. Or rather—_she_ talked. About Aralon’s art fairs, the salt air, the smell of rain. He listened. Asked good questions. Leaned in slightly when she got passionate about dragon-shaped pastries.

“You don’t talk much,” she teased, tracing the rim of her mug.

“I do,” he said. “Just... when it counts.”

Her expression softened. “That’s fair.”

Later, she pushed the Bloomleaf across the table. “Go on. Try it.”

He raised an eyebrow. “This isn’t some kind of trick, is it?”

“Only if you hate joy.”

He took a sip. Paused. Then carefully set it down.

“Was that...floral?” he asked, a little startled.

“Mmhmm.”

He nodded. “Like drinking a meadow.”

“So you love it?”

“Feels like I’m ”

She burst out laughing.

“Well, now you try _mine_,” he said, sliding his espresso across like a challenge.

She took one cautious sip, then winced like she bit into regret.

“It tastes like...schedules.”

“Exactly.”

And then—just to be silly—she poured a little of his espresso into her Bloomleaf. Stirred. Tasted. Froze.

He watched her. “Good or lethal?”

“Try it.”

He did.

They both blinked.

It was bold and soft, sharp and smooth. The salt drew out the chocolatey notes in the espresso. The cream mellowed the bite. It was like opposites agreeing—just for a moment—to hold hands.

Neither of them said anything for a long time.

Outside, the rain had slowed.

A little sign above the window glowed faintly in the lantern light:

> “Share the love. Please water the plant.”

She nudged the watering can on the floor gently toward him with her foot.

He picked it up.

Didn’t smile.

But...he did water the plant.
