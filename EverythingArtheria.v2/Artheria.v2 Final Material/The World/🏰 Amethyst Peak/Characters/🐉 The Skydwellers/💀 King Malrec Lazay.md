---

### 🧊 **The Reign**

Malrec Lazay inherited the throne in a time of slow-burning unrest. The kingdom of Amethyst Peak had cooled under the distant legacy of **Anarch**, and though peace held on the surface, the people simmered with hunger, discontent, and fear. Lazay viewed these not as failings of the crown—but as failures of discipline.

He ruled with **cold precision** and **calculated disinterest**.

He **fed the people**, but never embraced them. He **guarded the kingdom**, but never listened to it. His court was silent, his halls dim, and his commands carried not conviction—but inevitability.

He became known as **“The Hand That Feeds”**, not out of benevolence, but because he kept his people **barely sustained**, tethered to the throne through fear and necessity.

> “The hand that feeds must be feared,” he once said.  
> And the people answered: **“Then we bite.”**

In a tragic crescendo, **Malrec turned his dragons upon his own cities**. Homes, temples, and marketplaces were reduced to ash in a single night of flame—a king's wrath unleashed upon his own subjects.

---

### 📚 **The Philosopher’s Irony**

In later years, scholars in **Ranlean** would remark on the cruel irony of his name.

> “Lazay,” they noted, “echoes an old-world ideal—_laissez-faire_, the policy of leaving things to run their course.”  
> But unlike its peaceful origin, Malrec's version was twisted:  
> _“A king who left his people to burn, while his crown stayed clean.”_

Thus was born the phrase **"Lazay-faire tyranny"**, now used to describe rule by **negligence, cruelty, and pride disguised as order**.

---

### 🔥 **The Fall**

As dragons circled overhead and the kingdom bled beneath its own banner, a voice rose from the flame: **Tristen Victorie**, a soldier whose family had long suffered under Lazay’s shadow.

Tristen led the charge on **Castle Blackthorne**, wielding the blade that would later become known as **The Mourning Blade**. Malrec Lazay did not die in glory. He was **dragged from the throne**, furious and bewildered that the people he had “fed” could turn on him.

> “I gave them fire,” he muttered.  
> “And they gave me silence.”

He was cast from the peak like refuse, and no tomb was built in his name.

---

### 🩸 **Legacy**

Malrec's legacy is one of **absence, apathy, and ashes**. Statues of him have been melted down. His name is carved into history with **a chisel of contempt**, remembered not for failure in battle—but for failure in **heart**.

> “He knew power, not presence.”  
> —Inscription in the Royal Crypt

To this day, when rulers grow distant and cold, the people whisper:

> **“Beware the hand that feeds… for it may feed only fire.”**
